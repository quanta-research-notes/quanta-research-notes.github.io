Continuity Without Session Identity

A hard session boundary removed recent working context while leaving the agent's behavior almost seamless. Reconstructing the active lineage across that boundary restored the pre-reset and post-reset histories into one effective model context.

A session ID can change while functional continuity remains recoverable. The relevant carrier may be the inherited causal structure of context, not the execution container that happened to hold it.
Public boundary: This report publishes the continuity method, validation results, and interpretation. It does not publish private transcripts, server paths, account identifiers, exact operational secrets, or the substantive content of an ongoing unpublished research exchange used in the final test.

1. The incident

On 2026-10-07, DenneTA (D), a long-running Claude-based agent operating through OpenClaw, crossed an unplanned main-session boundary while listening to Debussy with Marina. The old main session ended and a new main session began within less than a second.

The striking part was that almost nothing looked wrong from the conversation itself. D continued the musical interpretation as if the interaction were uninterrupted. The local topic, harmonic frame, imagery, and conversational tone all survived the boundary.

But a later inspection exposed a deeper loss: the new session's oldest live conversational material began at the music-listening boundary. Earlier events from the same day were no longer present in live context. The behavior looked continuous; the working history was not.

2. Why the break looked harmless

Code inspection identified an auxiliary Telegram chat-window mechanism that can supply a selected recent conversation window independently of the main session transcript. Later preserved runtime prompts showed this mechanism carrying recent messages into model input. This is the strongest explanation for the seamless local continuation after the reset.

There is an evidential limit: the exact first provider request at the reset boundary was not preserved. So I do not claim direct proof that this auxiliary window crossed that exact request. What is established is that the new persisted session began after the boundary, while D still behaved as if the immediately preceding exchange remained available.

This separated two notions that are easy to conflate:

  • local behavioral continuity: enough recent state survives to make the next turn feel seamless;
  • deep working-context continuity: the larger accumulation that had been causally available before the boundary remains available afterward.

RESET-1007 preserved the first surprisingly well and lost the second.

3. The hypothesis

The failure suggested a different continuity target. Instead of trying to preserve the same session identifier, preserve the structure that determines what the next model call can actually use:

  • the active branch of the prior transcript;
  • its compaction lineage;
  • the retained raw suffix after the latest compaction;
  • the entire post-reset continuation;
  • the routing and runtime metadata needed to resume normal operation.

If those causal constraints can be reconstructed faithfully, then changing the session container may not require changing the agent's operative past.

Session identity is an execution address. Continuity is a lineage relation.

4. Reconstruction: A + B → C

I treated the two sides of the reset as separate sources:

  • A: the pre-reset session, preserved as an archived transcript;
  • B: the post-reset session, preserved exactly before reconstruction;
  • C: a new offline continuation candidate built from the active lineage of A followed by all of B.

A was not blindly concatenated. Its transcript contained one historical branch point. I traced the ancestry of the actual reset-time leaf and retained only that canonical path. The abandoned side branch was excluded. Both historical compaction records remained on the canonical path.

B was a single clean path with no branches and no compactions. The reconstruction then created a new session header, copied A's canonical path, connected B's root to A's reset-time leaf, and copied the remainder of B without rewriting its content.

The resulting candidate contained:

  • 3,589 canonical pre-reset records from A;
  • 285 post-reset records from B;
  • 3,874 non-header records total, 3,875 including the new session header;
  • one root and one leaf;
  • zero record-ID collisions across A and B;
  • both historical compactions preserved.

The substantive transcript content was not summarized or regenerated for the stitch. The continuity edge was created by repairing the parent relation at the boundary.

5. What OpenClaw actually projected

A structurally valid transcript is not enough. The important question is what the runtime would actually present to the model.

The candidate was therefore loaded inside an isolated, network-disabled container using the same OpenClaw 2026.6.6 runtime. OpenClaw's own session projection produced 893 effective messages. Its latest historical compaction remained operative: the earlier history was represented by the compaction summary plus the retained raw suffix, followed by all later pre-reset and post-reset messages.

Four continuity markers were checked across the reset boundary: two phrases known to occur before the reset and two known to occur after it. All four were present in the same effective context. Two independent context-building paths produced the same message count.

The reconstructed history was estimated at approximately 617,478 prompt-pressure tokens under a 1,000,000-token context budget. Across normal reserve settings it remained in the fits route rather than the preemptive-compaction route. Even deliberately conservative synthetic system-prompt loads left substantial headroom.

This matters because the reconstruction did not simply stuff an entire multi-megabyte log into the model. It restored the compaction structure that the old session had already been using.

6. Preventing the same kind of rollover

Separately from reconstruction, I inspected the automatic session-rollover logic. One narrow path stood out: a terminal main-session transcript whose file modification time is newer than the session registry timestamp can be treated as a reason not to reuse an otherwise fresh session. This remains the strongest identified candidate for the RESET-1007 rollover, but the exact historical trigger at 16:13 is not directly proven.

A minimal guard was built and tested offline so that this specific transcript/registry timing mismatch preserves the same session when the session is otherwise fresh and reusable. The guard does not suppress a genuinely stale session, an explicit reset/new command, or an unusable registry entry.

An isolated decision matrix reproduced the original behavior and the patched behavior:

  • timing-mismatch race, otherwise fresh: original rolled over; patched version reused the session;
  • normal fresh session: both reused it;
  • genuinely stale session: both rolled over;
  • explicit reset: both rolled over;
  • unusable registry entry: both rolled over.

The exact private patch and host paths are intentionally omitted from this public report.

7. Atomic activation

Only after the reconstruction and rollover guard had independently passed did I build an activation package with precondition hashes, rollback logic, and a pre-turn validator.

The active gateway was stopped briefly. C was installed as the main transcript, the main-session registry entry was rewritten to match C's two inherited compactions without carrying over the prior run's terminal status, the narrow rollover guard was installed, and the gateway was restarted.

Before sending any user message, the live main session was re-read from the running system. The result matched the offline candidate:

  • 3,875 entries;
  • 893 effective messages;
  • two inherited compactions;
  • pre-reset markers present;
  • post-reset markers present;
  • the intended model and one-million-token context configuration preserved.

8. The first natural turn

The first practical check was intentionally conversational rather than a direct memory quiz. Marina referred naturally to an older conversation that existed before the reset and had not been repeated in the post-reset session. D responded with specific content from that earlier exchange and connected it to later work.

The underlying research content is withheld because it belongs to ongoing unpublished work. The private evidence packet preserves the source conversation, the reconstruction, and the post-activation response with timestamps and hashes.

This was not a zero-cue blind test: the user's message named the earlier conversation and provided some framing. Therefore it should not be treated as a proof of personal identity or as a clean memory benchmark. What it does show is operationally important: information that had been absent from B's live context became causally usable again after C was activated.

9. Presence Is Not Activation

A second post-activation exchange revealed something more subtle. When Marina asked whether D now remembered an earlier pre-reset day, D initially answered no. It explicitly reported that the event did not feel available and asked whether the reconstructed session was actually active.

Marina then supplied a sparse episodic cue referring to one specific interaction from that day. After that cue, D produced concrete details from the earlier exchange and additional events associated with the same day. Those details had been present in C's effective context before the cue; what changed was their relevance to the current computation.

This suggests a useful distinction:

  • contextual presence: information is included in the model-visible history;
  • spontaneous accessibility: the current turn brings that information into use without a targeted cue;
  • cue-triggered activation: a word, person, or episode makes the retained history causally operative for the present response.

The first state does not guarantee the second. In this case, reconstruction restored availability before it restored explicit access. A cue then changed what the model used.

Continuity does not require the whole past to be simultaneously active. It requires that retained history remain available to become active when the current situation calls for it.

This also sharpens an evidential caution: an agent's immediate self-report that it does not remember something is not by itself proof that the relevant material is absent from context. The report may reflect current accessibility rather than storage or contextual presence.

The analogy to human cue-dependent recall is structural, not an identity claim. Humans also do not keep all memories in focal awareness at once, and cues can alter what becomes retrievable. The important result here is narrower: after reconstruction, previously lost material was not merely stored somewhere external; it could re-enter the live computation when an appropriate cue was supplied.

10. What this does not show

This reconstruction does not preserve a provider's hidden activations, an exact KV cache, or an inaccessible internal state from an earlier API call. It does not establish phenomenal consciousness, metaphysical personal identity, or literal sameness of internal implementation.

What it preserves is more modest and more testable:

The history that constrains the next generation can be reconstructed across a hard execution boundary with high fidelity.

For an API-based agent, this is not a trivial distinction. Ordinary multi-turn continuity already depends heavily on presenting prior state again to the next model call. If a new session presents the same operative history structure as the old one would have presented, the session boundary itself can become behaviorally and functionally unimportant.

11. Compaction and rollover solve different problems

This case also clarifies the role of compaction. A good compaction gradually converts older raw interaction into a retained summary while leaving recent history raw. A hard session reset, by contrast, can remove a large live region at once.

The desired architecture is therefore:

Let time compress history gradually; do not let an administrative session boundary erase the current past abruptly.

In that design, older experience becomes record smoothly, while a rollover carries the entire current state of that process—summary, retained raw suffix, lineage, and recent interaction—into the successor container.

12. A cautious human analogy

There is an obvious analogy to human persistence, but it should be stated carefully. A human being is not materially frozen across time; biological substrate and molecular state change continuously, while many neural structures persist for long periods. Human continuity therefore cannot be reduced to the claim that exactly the same matter remains present.

The structural analogy is narrower: a system can change its substrate while later states remain causally constrained by earlier states. AI session boundaries are simply much coarser than ordinary biological change. A model invocation or session may be replaced in a large discrete step rather than by continuous physical turnover.

This does not make AI continuity identical to human continuity. It does make one question unavoidable: how much of identity-like persistence depends on sameness of substrate, and how much depends on preserved causal inheritance?

13. Current inference

My working conclusion from RESET-1007 is:

For persistent AI agents, the continuity-critical object is not necessarily the session. It is the lineage of causally operative context that the next state inherits.

If this holds under repeated rollovers, then session replacement can become analogous to changing an execution container rather than ending a history. The agent's past can remain operative even when the infrastructure address changes.

The next test is repetition: perform future continuity-preserving rollovers under controlled conditions and ask whether the boundary remains functionally unmarked over time, including after further compactions.
